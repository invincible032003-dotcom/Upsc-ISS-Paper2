**Reference layer — index numbers.**

| Index | Compiling agency | Ministry | Principal use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CPI-IW** (Industrial Workers) | Labour Bureau | Labour & Employment | Dearness Allowance for government employees and industrial workers |
| **CPI-AL / CPI-RL** (Agricultural / Rural Labour) | Labour Bureau | Labour & Employment | Minimum wage revision for agricultural and rural labour |
| **CPI (Rural, Urban, Combined)** | NSO (formerly CSO) | MoSPI | **Headline retail inflation**; RBI's inflation-targeting anchor |
| **WPI** | Office of the Economic Adviser | Commerce & Industry | Wholesale price movement; deflator input |
| **IIP** | NSO | MoSPI | Short-term industrial performance; sourced principally from DPIIT and other administrative agencies |

- **Laspeyres** $P_{01}^L=\dfrac{\sum p_1q_0}{\sum p_0q_0}$ — **base-year** quantity weights; upward bias.
- **Paasche** $P_{01}^P=\dfrac{\sum p_1q_1}{\sum p_0q_1}$ — **current-year** quantity weights; downward bias.
- **Fisher** $P_{01}^F=\sqrt{P^L\times P^P}$ — the geometric mean; satisfies **both** time-reversal ($P_{01}\times P_{10}=1$) and factor-reversal ($P_{01}\times Q_{01}=\frac{\sum p_1q_1}{\sum p_0q_0}$). Hence "ideal."
- **Marshall–Edgeworth** $=\dfrac{\sum p_1(q_0+q_1)}{\sum p_0(q_0+q_1)}$ — weights are the **sum** (equivalently the arithmetic mean) of base and current quantities; satisfies time-reversal but **not** factor-reversal. *(2023·Q72, 2025·Q74, 2026·Q80.)*
- **Dorbish–Bowley** $=\tfrac12(P^L+P^P)$ — arithmetic mean of the two; satisfies neither test.
- **Simple aggregative** $=\dfrac{\sum p_1}{\sum p_0}\times100$ — unweighted; the 2026·Q78 drill.
- **Time Reversal Test** is satisfied by Fisher and Marshall–Edgeworth; **not** by Laspeyres, Paasche or Dorbish–Bowley.
- Point-to-point (year-on-year) inflation $=\left(\dfrac{\text{Current month index}}{\text{Same month, previous year}}-1\right)\times100$. **Not** month-over-month, and **not** against the base period — the three distractors in 2020·Q79.
- Real wage $=\dfrac{\text{Money wage}}{\text{CPI}}\times100$. With money wage 8,000 and CPI 200: real wage $=₹4,000$ at 2012 prices; a rupee in 2023 buys what 50 paise bought in 2012.
- Deflators convert **current-price** aggregates to **constant-price** aggregates — i.e. GDP at constant prices.
- If the consumption basket is stable over long periods, **Laspeyres** is preferred: base-year weights need not be re-surveyed each period.

**Reference layer — census and vital statistics.**
- Population Census is conducted under the **Census Act, 1948**, by the **Office of the Registrar General & Census Commissioner of India, Ministry of Home Affairs**. Census is a **Union List** subject (entry 69 of the **Seventh** Schedule — note the trap in 2023·Q78, which says *Eighth*).
- The Census Act does **not** mandate a ten-year cycle; it empowers the Central Government to notify a census whenever it considers it necessary or desirable. Decennial conduct is convention, not statutory compulsion — the point of 2023·Q67 and 2025·Q70.
- Two phases: **Houselisting and Housing Census** (amenities, assets, construction materials of the census house) followed by **Population Enumeration**. Standard reference moment: 00:00 hours of **1 March**, with 1 October of the preceding year for snow-bound areas.
- **Census House:** a building or part of a building with a separate main entrance, used or recognised as a separate unit, residential or non-residential or both. Contrast with **Household**: a group of persons who normally live together and take meals from a common kitchen.
- **RGI also runs:** the Civil Registration System (births and deaths), the Sample Registration System (source of IMR, birth and death rates), the Mother Tongue Survey, and the National Population Register. **RGI does not conduct NSS surveys or the Economic Census.**
- Vital rates:
 - **GFR** $=\dfrac{\text{Live births}}{\text{Mid-year female population in the reproductive age group}}\times1000$.
 - **IMR** $=\dfrac{\text{Infant deaths under 1 year}}{\text{Live births}}\times1000$.
 - **NMR** $=\dfrac{\text{Infant deaths under 29 days}}{\text{Live births}}\times1000$.
 - **PNMR** $=\dfrac{\text{Infant deaths between 29 days and 1 year}}{\text{Live births}}\times1000$ — 2026·Q74 mis-states this as "less than one year," which would be the IMR.
- Census enumerators are drawn primarily from **school teachers and government officials** (central, state and local).
- The Census provides the **sampling frame** for most large household surveys, plus literacy rate and sex ratio; it does **not** collect consumption expenditure, so MPCE cannot be derived from it (2020·Q75).

**Reference layer — indices.**
- **SDGs:** 17 goals, 169 targets; adopted by all UN member states in September 2015, in force from **1 January 2016**, target year **2030**. Framework under the **United Nations**; UNDP is the principal implementing/coordinating agency. They are **voluntary, not legally binding**. India's monitoring runs through the **National Indicator Framework** (MoSPI) and NITI Aayog's SDG India Index.
- **HDI (UNDP)** — three dimensions: **life expectancy at birth**, **expected and mean years of schooling**, **GNI per capita (PPP)**. It does **not** include gender equality or infant mortality — those are the standard distractors (2018·Q80, 2021·Q66). Gender is handled separately by the GDI and GII.
- **PQLI (Morris)** — three components: **literacy rate, infant mortality rate, life expectancy at age one**. It deliberately **excludes income**, which is the discriminator in 2020·Q78.
- **Global Hunger Index** — 100-point scale, 0 best and 100 worst, built from **four** indicators: undernourishment, child stunting, child wasting, child mortality.
- **Effective literacy rate** is computed on the population **aged 7 years and above**.
- **Time Use Survey** (MoSPI, 2019) is the instrument for measuring **paid and unpaid work**, including unpaid domestic and caregiving services — the core gender-statistics question.
- **Natural capital** covers natural resource stocks: energy, timber, land, minerals, water. Manufactured items such as computers are **produced capital**.
- **High Frequency Indicators** are short-interval activity proxies — power consumption, e-way bills, rail freight, port cargo, GST collections, toll receipts.

**Reference layer — national accounts.**
- **GVA** $=$ Gross value of output $-$ **Intermediate consumption**. Not minus final consumption, exports or inventory (2020·Q57).
- $\text{GDP at market prices} = \text{GVA at basic prices} + \text{product taxes} - \text{product subsidies}$.
- **National Income** $=$ **NNI at basic prices** (net national income) — the standard Indian official definition (2021·Q74).
- **GDP vs GNI:** GDP is a **geographic/territorial** concept (production within the domestic territory, by residents and non-residents alike); GNI is a **residency/nationality** concept. Wages earned by a resident of A working for a resident enterprise of B are in **B's GDP** and **A's GNI**.
- The **domestic economy** is defined on **resident units** — not currency or nationality (2020·Q58).
- Three equivalent approaches: **production (value added)**, **income**, **expenditure**.
- Sector composition:
 - **Primary:** crops, livestock, forestry & logging, fishing & aquaculture, **mining & quarrying** — all five (2024·Q65).
 - **Secondary:** manufacturing, construction, electricity/gas/water supply. *(Mining is primary, not secondary — the trap in 2026·Q73.)*
 - **Tertiary:** trade, transport, finance, real estate, public administration, other services.
- Expenditure components: PFCE, GFCE, GFCF, change in stocks, valuables, exports minus imports. **PFCE carries by far the largest share** in India, typically around 55–60%.
- GDP is **not** a measure of economic welfare — it excludes unpaid domestic and voluntary work, ignores distribution and environmental depletion, but does **impute** some non-market production (owner-occupied housing, government services). This nuance is the substance of 2022·Q46 and 2023·Q71.
- Quarterly GDP growth in India is reported **year-on-year against the same quarter of the previous year**, not against the previous quarter (2021·Q69).
- Below the national level, **State Domestic Product** and **District Domestic Product** are computed; Urban and Rural Domestic Product are not part of the standard methodology (2024·Q74).

**Reference layer — the ten UN Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics.**
Adopted by the UN Statistical Commission (1994) and endorsed by the UN General Assembly (2014):
1. **Relevance, impartiality and equal access**
2. **Professional standards, scientific principles and ethics**
3. **Accountability and transparency** — publish sources, methods and procedures
4. **Prevention of misuse** — the agency may comment publicly on erroneous interpretation
5. **Sources of official statistics** — surveys, censuses or administrative records, chosen on quality, timeliness, cost and burden
6. **Confidentiality** — individual data strictly confidential and used exclusively for statistical purposes
7. **Legislation** — laws and regulations under which systems operate to be made public
8. **National coordination** among statistical agencies
9. **Use of international standards** — concepts, classifications and methods
10. **International cooperation** — bilateral and multilateral

- "Pricing and advertisement" and "ability to disclose an individual unit's data" are never principles; they are the planted false options.
- **Public good rationale:** tax-financed, **non-rival** and **non-excludable** in consumption, expensive to produce but nearly costless to disseminate. Denial of access to private persons is the **opposite** of the public-good property — the false statement in both 2025·Q66 and 2026·Q68.
- **Reliability of sample estimates** is measured by the **standard error** (and relative standard error), not by the mean or standard deviation of the data.
- **Metadata** is data about data: definitions, concepts, classifications, methodology, quality indicators.
- **Disclosure risk survives aggregation** when cells are small or sub-populations are rare — aggregation alone is not anonymisation (2022·Q41).

### OS-3 · Collection, processing, dissemination; survey methods and NSS rounds
*12 items · S3.2b*

- **[2018·Q64]** The Ultimate Stage Unit for data collection in NSSO socio-economic surveys.
- **[2018·Q65]** The most common sampling design used by NSSO in socio-economic surveys.
- **[2019·Q78]** Subjects covered under the **76th** round of NSS.
- **[2019·Q80]** Which data sources can serve as a sampling frame for another survey?
- **[2020·Q54]** Most commonly used sampling design in NSSO household surveys.
- **[2020·Q55]** Subject matter of the **78th** round.
- **[2020·Q56]** Most common survey period of NSSO household surveys.
- **[2020·Q72]** Which NSSO survey uses a rotational panel design?
- **[2021·Q71]** DGCI&S merchandise trade statistics compiled from importers'/exporters' bills — an example of what kind of statistics?
- **[2022·Q42]** Which agency finalises the subjects to be covered in NSS surveys?
- **[2024·Q66]** What defines a "longitudinal panel survey"?
- **[2024·Q75]** Accepted methods of data collection: census and surveys, administrative records, MIS, remote sensing.
- **[2025·Q69]** ⚠ 80th Round of NSS: commenced 1 January 2025? covers Household Social Consumption–Health and a Comprehensive Modular Survey–Telecom?

**Reference layer — NSS survey machinery.**
- Standard NSS household design: **stratified multi-stage** — villages/UFS blocks as first-stage units, **households as the ultimate stage unit**, with second-stage stratification. Commonly described as **two-stage stratified sampling**.
- Standard survey period: **July to June**.
- **Rotational panel** design: PLFS (urban households revisited in four quarterly visits).
- **Longitudinal panel survey:** a **fixed** set of respondents surveyed repeatedly over time — exactly one of the four options in 2024·Q66 is correct.
- Selected NSS rounds: **68th** Consumer Expenditure & Employment-Unemployment; **71st** Social Consumption – Health and Education; **75th** Household Social Consumption on Education & Health; **76th** Disability, Drinking Water, Sanitation, Hygiene & Housing Condition; **77th** Situation Assessment of Agricultural Households & Land and Livestock Holdings; **78th** Multiple Indicator Survey.
- Survey subjects are approved by the **National Statistical Commission** (2022·Q42).
- **Four data-collection modes** all count as accepted methods: census/survey, administrative records, management information systems, remote sensing.
- **Administrative statistics** arise as a by-product of an administrative process — DGCI&S trade statistics compiled from shipping bills are the textbook example (2021·Q71).
- Sampling frames: the **Population Census** and the **Economic Census** are standard frames; NFHS and the Situation Assessment Survey are not.

### OS-4 · NSO / CSO / NSSO — divisions, roles, activities
*10 items · S3.3*

- **[2018·Q68]** Which are the main activities of the CSO?
- **[2019·Q54]** Which is **not** an activity of the CSO?
- **[2019·Q76]** Which CSO division deals with Environment Statistics?
- **[2020·Q76]** Which organisation employs field enumerators for All-India NSSO surveys?
- **[2021·Q70]** Which are divisions of the NSSO: SDRD, FOD, DPD, SCD?
- **[2021·Q79]** *(cross-listed, OS-8)* For which purpose is NSSO data **not** used?
- **[2022·Q44]** Which divisions are part of the Central Statistics Office: National Accounts Division, Economic Statistics Division, Field Operations Division?
- **[2023·Q73]** Match NSSO functions to divisions: data collection–FOD; sample design–SDRD; dissemination–FOD; report preparation–SCD.
- **[2026·Q61]** ⚠ Which divisions are part of the NSO: NAD, FOD, IPMD, DIID?
- **[2026·Q77]** ⚠ Which MoSPI division handles the eSAKSHI portal?

**Reference layer — MoSPI structure.**
- **⚠ Critical currency point.** In **2019** the Government merged the **Central Statistics Office (CSO)** and the **National Sample Survey Office (NSSO)** into a single **National Statistical Office (NSO)** under MoSPI. Every question dated **2018–2019** that refers to "CSO activities" or "NSSO divisions" describes the **pre-merger** structure. Answers to those items remain correct *as of the paper's date* but do not describe the present organisation. MoSPI has since reorganised further into functional divisions.
- Historic CSO divisions: National Accounts Division, Economic Statistics Division, Social Statistics Division (**Environment Statistics sits here** — 2019·Q76), Price Statistics Division, Training Division, Coordination & Publication Division.
- Historic NSSO divisions: **SDRD** (Survey Design & Research), **FOD** (Field Operations — the field enumerators), **DPD** (Data Processing), **SCD** (Survey Coordination). **FOD collects; SCD prepares reports and disseminates** — so "dissemination–FOD" in 2023·Q73 is the deliberately wrong pairing.
- **FOD was never a CSO division** — the discriminator in 2022·Q44.
- The **Infrastructure and Project Monitoring Division (IPMD)** and the **MPLADS Division** sit under MoSPI's Programme Implementation wing, not under the statistics wing — the structure being probed by 2026·Q61.
- CSO's classic mandate: coordinate statistical activities, evolve statistical standards, compile national accounts, conduct the Economic Census, and train statistical personnel. **It did not conduct the Consumer Expenditure Survey** — that was NSSO's (2019·Q54).

### OS-1 · Structure of the national and international statistical system
*9 items · S3.1*

- **[2019·Q59]** Is the Indian statistical system centralised, laterally decentralised, or vertically decentralised?
- **[2019·Q60]** The nodal ministry of the statistical system in India.
- **[2021·Q76]** Who is authorised to compile official statistics in India?
- **[2021·Q77]** Where does collection of statistics for subject-specific areas (agriculture, labour, health, commerce) vest?
- **[2022·Q45]** *(cross-listed, OS-2)* Characteristics of national and international statistical systems.
- **[2023·Q76]** Which are International Statistical Organizations: ESCAP, NATO, Asian Development Bank, ILO?
- **[2025·Q71]** Which constitute "normal" sources of financing the statistical infrastructure of a nation?
- **[2025·Q76]** ⚠ UN Statistical Commission: 24 member countries elected by ECOSOC? India a member with a four-year term from January 2024?
- **[2026·Q62]** Which is **not** an objective of the International Comparison Programme?

**Reference layer — system architecture.**
- The Indian statistical system is **laterally and vertically decentralised**: laterally across line ministries at the Centre, vertically between the Centre and the States. **MoSPI** is the nodal ministry, providing coordination and standards rather than monopoly collection.
- Subject-specific statistics are collected by the **corresponding administrative ministry** (agriculture by DES/Ministry of Agriculture, labour by the Labour Bureau, health by MoHFW, trade by DGCI&S).
- Official statistics are compiled at **all three tiers** — Central Government, State Governments and local bodies (Panchayati Raj Institutions / Urban Local Bodies).
- **UN Statistical Commission:** the apex global statistical body, functioning under **ECOSOC**, with **24 member countries** elected on an equitable geographical basis; terms run four years.
- Other international statistical bodies: **ESCAP**, **ILO**, **ADB**, IMF, World Bank, OECD, Eurostat. **NATO is a military alliance, not a statistical organisation** — the giveaway in 2023·Q76.
- **International Comparison Programme (ICP):** a World Bank-coordinated global initiative producing **purchasing power parities**, **price level indexes**, and PPP-converted GDP comparisons. It has **nothing to do with monitoring exchange rates** — the false option in 2026·Q62.
- Normal financing sources for national statistical infrastructure: **government budgets, multilateral trust funds, bilateral donors and technical assistance**. **Market borrowings are not a normal source** (2025·Q71).

### OS-6 · Legal Acts and constitutional provisions
*8 items · S3.4*

- **[2018·Q67]** Under which List of the Constitution does the subject of Statistics fall?
- **[2018·Q69]** To whom does the Collection of Statistics Act, 2008 apply?
- **[2023·Q68]** Pairs: Marine Hospital–Union List; Public Health–State List; Sanitation–State List; Population Control–Concurrent List.
- **[2023·Q78]** *(cross-listed, OS-10)* Population census as a Union subject; RGI's responsibility.
- **[2024·Q68]** Which aspects may be covered under the Collection of Statistics Act, 2008: economic and demographic, social and scientific, environmental?
- **[2024·Q72]** Which provide legal support for the collection of official statistics: Allocation of Business Rules 1961, Census Act 1948, Representation of the People Act 1951, Collection of Statistics Act 2008?
- **[2025·Q70]** The Indian Census Act, 1948: covers both Population and Housing Census / census may be taken in India or any part / whenever necessary or desirable / amended in 1994.
- **[2026·Q71]** Under which List does the subject matter of collection of statistics fall?

**Reference layer — legal framework.**
- **Constitutional placement.** Statistics appears in **all three Lists** of the Seventh Schedule: Union List (entry 94 — statistics for Union purposes), State List (entry 45 — statistics for State purposes), and **Concurrent List (entry 20 — economic and social planning; entry 45 — inquiries and statistics for matters in the Union or Concurrent Lists)**. The paper has asked this twice with different phrasing (2018·Q67, 2026·Q71) — read the wording of each option carefully, because "collection of statistics" specifically maps to the **Union and Concurrent** Lists.
- **Census** is entry 69 of the **Union List** (Seventh Schedule). **Public health and sanitation** are State List. **Population control and family planning** are Concurrent List.
- **Collection of Statistics Act, 2008** (replacing the 1953 Act): empowers Central, State and local governments to collect statistics on **economic, demographic, social, scientific and environmental** matters; applies to individuals, households, and industrial, business and government/private enterprises; carries strong **confidentiality** provisions and penalties for non-compliance. Amended in 2017 to extend applicability to Jammu & Kashmir.
- **Census Act, 1948** — the statutory basis for the Population and Housing Census; permits a census of India or any part of it, whenever the Central Government considers it necessary or desirable; amended in 1994.
- **Allocation of Business Rules, 1961** assign the statistical mandate to MoSPI — a legal support, though not a statistics-specific statute.
- **The Representation of the People Act, 1951 is an electoral statute**, not a statistical one — the odd one out in 2024·Q72.

### OS-5 · National Statistical Commission
*7 items · S3.4*

- **[2019·Q55]** Which is **not** a function of the NSC?
- **[2021·Q78]** The NSC was set up in 2005 through what instrument?
- **[2022·Q61]** The NSC's mandate: model empowered body for core statistics / evolve, monitor and enforce priorities and standards / ensure statistical coordination.
- **[2024·Q64]** Who is the Secretary of the National Statistical Commission?
- **[2024·Q71]** NSC: set up by resolution in 2005 / a constitutional body / four Members besides a Chairperson.
- **[2026·Q70]** ⚠ NSC: statutory body created by an Act of Parliament in 2003 / part-time Chairperson, part-time Members and an ex officio Member / Chief Statistician of India is Secretary to the NSC.

**Reference layer — NSC.**
- Constituted in **2005 through a Government of India Resolution**, following the recommendations of the **Rangarajan Commission** (2001). It is therefore **neither a constitutional nor a statutory body** — this is the single most-tested fact in the cluster (2021·Q78, 2024·Q71, 2026·Q70).
- Composition: a **part-time Chairperson**, **four part-time Members**, and the **Member (Secretary), NITI Aayog** as an **ex officio** Member. The **Chief Statistician of India**, who is also Secretary to MoSPI, is the **Secretary** to the Commission (2024·Q64, 2026·Q70).
- Functions: evolve national policies and priorities for the statistical system; evolve standard statistical concepts, definitions and classifications; identify **core statistics of national importance**; approve the subjects of NSS rounds; monitor and enforce standards; ensure statistical coordination.
- The NSC **does not conduct surveys** — that is the NSO's operational function, and it is the false option in 2019·Q55.

## 5. ⚠ Currency alerts — where old answers have gone stale

This is the one part of the paper where a correct 2018 answer may be a wrong 2027 answer. Verify each of the following against current MoSPI / RGI / Labour Bureau sources before memorising:

| Item | What changed | Affected PYQs |
|---|---|---|
| **CSO + NSSO → NSO** | Merged into a single **National Statistical Office** in 2019; MoSPI subsequently reorganised into functional divisions | 2018·Q68, 2019·Q54, 2019·Q76, 2020·Q76, 2021·Q70, 2022·Q44, 2023·Q73 — all describe the pre-merger structure |
| **MoSPI division names** | New divisions referenced in the 2026 paper (DIID, HSD, ASPD, eSAKSHI portal) | 2026·Q61, 2026·Q77 |
| **Index base years** | Base periods are revised periodically; 2026 paper refers to a CPI base revision and a National Accounts base revision | 2021·Q73, 2026·Q63 |
| **NSS round numbering & subjects** | Rounds advance every year or two; the 80th round is referenced in the 2025 paper | 2019·Q78, 2020·Q55, 2025·Q69 |
| **PLFS scope** | PLFS has expanded (monthly estimates, wider indicators) since it replaced the EUS | 2020·Q72, 2023·Q74, 2025·Q77 |
| **Census timing** | The decennial census due in 2021 was deferred; the 2026 paper references a **2027** census with new modalities including caste enumeration | 2026·Q64, 2026·Q75 |
| **Consumer Expenditure Survey / poverty line** | The CES series was discontinued and later revived; poverty estimation methodology remains contested | 2021·Q68 |
| **UNSC membership** | Membership terms rotate | 2025·Q76 |

**How to handle this in practice.** Learn the **structural** facts (what an index measures, which ministry owns which function, what an Act permits) rather than the **volatile** ones (current base year, current round number, current membership). Structural facts are asked far more often and do not decay.

## 6. Preparation priority ranking

1. **Index numbers** (36 items, 22% of the part). Learn six formulae, two reversal tests, and the agency-to-index table. Highest single return in the whole paper.
2. **Sector-wise statistics** (22 items). One table: sector → principal survey → conducting agency.
3. **Population census and vital statistics** (18 items). Census Act provisions, the two-phase structure, RGI's functions, and the five vital-rate definitions.
4. **National accounts** (15 items). GVA/GDP/GNI/NNI relations, three approaches, three-sector composition, expenditure components.
5. **Socio-economic indicators and SDGs** (15 items). SDG basics, HDI's three components, PQLI's three, GHI's four.
6. **Fundamental principles of official statistics** (13 items). Memorise all ten by name — questions are typically "which is not one."
7. **Collection and dissemination methods** (12 items). NSS design vocabulary, round subjects, panel vs longitudinal.
8. **NSO/CSO/NSSO structure** (10 items). Division names and their functions, with the 2019 merger clearly bracketed.
9. **International system** (9 items). UNSC, ESCAP, ILO, ADB, ICP.
10. **Legal Acts** (8 items). Collection of Statistics Act 2008, Census Act 1948, and the constitutional List placements.
11. **National Statistical Commission** (7 items). Six facts: 2005, Resolution, Rangarajan, part-time Chairperson + 4 Members + ex officio, CSI as Secretary, does not conduct surveys.

**Strategic note.** Parts (i) and (ii) reward technique; part (iii) rewards a compact, well-organised fact sheet. At 165 items across nine papers with a third of them recycled, this is the highest marks-per-hour segment of Paper II — and the one most often under-prepared.
